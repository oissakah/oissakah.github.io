Earlier computational research

Materials informatics, screening, and battery modeling

Project 01

High-throughput screening of porous catalysts

The design space of metal–organic frameworks and related porous materials is too large to examine candidate-by-candidate with expensive calculations alone. This project explored how learned crystal representations and computational screening can reduce that search space for photoelectrochemical CO₂-reduction applications.

Scientific question: how can structural information from a crystalline porous material be converted into a useful representation for fast property prediction and candidate prioritization?
Methods
Crystal graph neural networksCGCNNMonte Carlo methodsStructural descriptorsHigh-throughput screeningInterpretability

The emphasis was not only on ranking candidates, but on understanding which structural information influenced the screening decisions.

Workflow

Crystal structure → learned representation → screening decision

01 · Represent

Crystal graphs

Encode atoms and their local structural environments as a graph rather than relying only on hand-selected composition variables.

02 · Predict

Learn structure–property relationships

Use graph-based models and complementary computational methods to estimate properties relevant to screening.

03 · Prioritize

Reduce the candidate space

Use predicted properties, structural descriptors, and interpretability analysis to identify candidates for more detailed evaluation.

Project 02

ML-assisted screening of sodium-ion solid electrolytes

Solid-electrolyte discovery requires balancing several material attributes, including ionic transport and stability. Rather than applying costly simulation uniformly across a large candidate pool, this project used materials informatics to prioritize materials before more detailed atomistic analysis.

Scientific question: can lower-cost descriptors and predictive models identify the candidates most worth advancing to expensive atomistic calculations?
Methods
Materials descriptorsMachine learningAtomistic simulationCandidate screeningUncertainty-aware selectionSodium-ion batteries

This work strengthened my interest in multi-stage computational workflows where different levels of model fidelity are used for different parts of the search problem.

Screening logic

Use expensive computation where it adds the most value

01 · Describe

Build candidate features

Represent candidate materials using descriptors that capture composition and structure in a machine-learning-ready form.

02 · Rank

Prioritize candidates

Use predictive models and uncertainty-aware selection to narrow the candidate pool rather than treating every material equally.

03 · Validate

Advance selected materials

Apply more detailed atomistic simulation to candidates that remain promising after lower-cost screening.

Project 03

LiMn₂O₄ cathode modeling and synthesis

This project connected computational battery modeling with an experimentally synthesized material. Multiphase porous-electrode modeling was used to study electrochemical response, while porous LiMn₂O₄ microspheres were prepared experimentally by solvothermal synthesis.

Why it mattered for my development: it reinforced that models are most useful when their state variables and assumptions can be related back to a physical material and an experimentally measurable response.

Connection to current work

What carried forward into my PhD research

Graph representations

CGCNN introduced me to representing materials as graphs. My current nanonecklace work extends that idea into an explicitly physical, voltage-dependent electrical graph.

Multi-fidelity screening

Solid-electrolyte screening developed the idea of using fast models for exploration and expensive methods for validation. The same principle now appears in my PFAS catalysis workflow with ML interatomic potentials and DFT.

Mechanistic interpretation

Battery modeling reinforced the importance of connecting mathematical state variables to measurable physical behavior—a central principle in my current physics-informed modeling.
