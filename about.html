About

Materials engineering, computation, and the search for mechanism

I am a materials engineer from Ghana and a PhD researcher at the University of Nebraska–Lincoln. My research has evolved from experimental materials development toward computational models that explain and predict complex material behavior.

Academic journey

I studied Materials Engineering at the Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology (KNUST), graduating with First Class Honors in 2023. My undergraduate work on palm-kernel-shell/HDPE composites introduced me to the challenge of connecting composition, microstructure, processing, and performance.

That question expanded into materials informatics, battery modeling, porous-material screening, computational catalysis, and graph-based transport. In 2026 I began my PhD in Chemical and Biomolecular Engineering at the University of Nebraska–Lincoln, where my current work combines physics-based computation, machine learning, graph methods, and atomistic simulation.

Research identity

Computational materials & physics-informed AI

I am especially interested in problems where the measured response hides a rich internal state. My goal is to build models that make that hidden state visible and then use it to improve prediction and design.

How my research connects

The application areas differ, but the questions are closely related.

Structure

What is the material?

Atomic structure, network topology, composition, interfaces, and microstructure define the state space.

Mechanism

Why does it respond?

Physics-based models expose activation, transport, adsorption, connectivity, and redistribution mechanisms.

Prediction

How can we design faster?

Machine learning and surrogate models accelerate exploration once the relevant structure and physics are identified.
