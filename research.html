Research

Research program

I work at the intersection of materials physics, machine learning, and atomistic simulation. The common goal is to build models that expose the mechanisms behind material behavior and then use those mechanisms to accelerate design.

Current research thrusts

Nanomaterials · Graph physics · GNN

Emergent transport in self-assembled nanonecklace networks

How does voltage reorganize the conducting state of a disordered nanoparticle network?

I represent the material as an evolving active graph, solve the electrical state with Kirchhoff physics, and track the internal variables hidden from a two-terminal measurement: conducting components, nodal potentials, edge currents, connectivity, and current redistribution.

The project is being extended with graph neural-network surrogates that learn the system response while retaining access to network-level structure.

Catalysis · DFT · MLIPs

Computational catalyst design for PFAS remediation

Which atomic-scale surface environments govern adsorption and activation?

I use atomistic models of PFAS molecules on metal surfaces to study adsorption energetics, surface-site effects, and reaction-relevant descriptors. Machine-learned interatomic potentials are used to accelerate exploration, with first-principles calculations providing validation.

The aim is to connect local atomic structure to catalytic behavior and develop efficient screening workflows for environmental catalysis.

Materials informatics · Screening

Machine learning for materials discovery

How can predictive models reduce expensive search without becoming black boxes?

My broader materials-informatics work includes graph learning, classical ML, high-throughput screening, uncertainty-aware selection, and interpretability for structure–property relationships.

Applications include solid electrolytes, porous catalysts, sustainable composites, and nanomaterial transport.

Scientific computing · HPC

Mechanistic computation at scale

How can simulation workflows remain physically interpretable while scaling to larger design spaces?

I build reproducible scientific workflows in Python and on HPC systems, combining numerical solvers, atomistic simulation, graph analysis, GPU-accelerated learning, and publication-quality diagnostics.

Methods include VASP, ASE, PyTorch, NetworkX, SLURM, NumPy/SciPy, and custom physics-based solvers.

Research philosophy

Prediction is most useful when it reveals mechanism

Across projects, I am most interested in models that answer two questions at once: what will the material do? and why does it do it? That is why I combine machine learning with explicit physical state variables, atomistic structure, graph topology, and mechanistic simulation.
